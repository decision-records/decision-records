## Checklist: does this decision need a decision record?

Write one if you answer **yes** to any of these:

* [ ] Would reversing this decision be costly, slow, or embarrassing?
* [ ] Does it affect people outside the room where it's being decided?
* [ ] Is it likely someone will ask "why did we do it this way?" a year from now?
* [ ] Are there genuinely contested options, with reasonable people on different sides?
* [ ] Does it set a precedent or policy other decisions will need to be consistent with?
* [ ] Would a new hire or a new leader in this role benefit from knowing the reasoning?

Skip a decision record if **all** of these are true:

* [ ] It's cheap and fast to reverse.
* [ ] It affects only the decision-maker or a single self-contained team.
* [ ] It's already fully covered by existing policy, standard, or documentation.
* [ ] It's explicitly temporary — a workaround, pilot, or time-boxed experiment.

## Checklist: before you start writing

* [ ] Named decision-maker(s) identified — one person, or a specific committee/rule for how the group decides.
* [ ] Real stakeholders identified — who needs to weigh in before this is final?
* [ ] Deadline or urgency understood — does this have to be decided now, or can it wait for more information?
* [ ] Chose lightweight vs. comprehensive template based on stakes (see [template/index.md](../template/index.md#choosing-which-template-to-use)).
* [ ] Know where the finished record will be stored, so it isn't lost in a chat thread or a single person's inbox.

## Checklist: while writing

* [ ] Title is short, present-tense, and active — states the decision, not the topic.
* [ ] Context explains the situation to a reader with zero meeting background.
* [ ] At least two genuinely viable options are listed, including "do nothing" if relevant.
* [ ] Each option's trade-offs are stated as facts, not as a strawman built to make the favored option win.
* [ ] Decision is written in plain active voice: "We will do X" — not a pros/cons summary standing in for a decision.
* [ ] Consequences describe what changes next, not a rehash of the options debate.
* [ ] Related decisions, requirements, or documents are linked, if any exist.
* [ ] The whole thing focuses on exactly one decision — split it if you find two.

## Checklist: before you publish for comment

* [ ] A stranger to the discussion could read it and understand both the decision and why.
* [ ] No unresolved "did you consider X?" gaps — if a likely question isn't answered, add it.
* [ ] Status is set correctly (usually `request for comments` or `proposed` at this stage).
* [ ] Comment deadline and process are stated explicitly (e.g., "comments by Friday, then default-accepted").
* [ ] The record is one page or close to it — if it's sprawling, check whether it's actually more than one decision.

## Checklist: review and approval

* [ ] Every named stakeholder had a real chance to comment, not just a courtesy CC.
* [ ] Substantive comments are reflected in the record (as edits or as an added, dated note) — not just acknowledged and ignored.
* [ ] The named decision-maker explicitly accepted, rejected, or sent it back — silence is not acceptance.
* [ ] Status field is updated to match the outcome (`accepted`, `rejected`, etc.) with a date.
* [ ] If rejected or deferred, the record still stays in the log rather than being deleted — it documents that the question was raised and why it didn't proceed.

## Checklist: after the decision (rollout)

* [ ] Decision is communicated to everyone it affects, not just those who were in the room.
* [ ] Follow-on requirements, budget, or training implications from the "Consequences" section are actually assigned to someone.
* [ ] The record is filed in the shared decision log, not left in a personal folder or a chat thread.
* [ ] An accountable owner is named for future review of this decision.
* [ ] If this decision supersedes a previous one, the old record's status is updated to `superseded` with a link forward.

## Checklist: running decision records across an organization

* [ ] Decided who can *propose* a decision record (commonly: anyone).
* [ ] Decided who can *approve* one, and whether that varies by decision type, dollar threshold, or risk level.
* [ ] Decided the escalation path when stakeholders disagree.
* [ ] Chosen a storage location every relevant team will actually use.
* [ ] Chosen a naming convention people can adopt without a program manager — "decisions" is often more approachable than "decision records."
* [ ] Identified a small number of early, high-visibility decisions to record first, to demonstrate value before mandating anything.
* [ ] Avoided turning this into forced paperwork — introduced it as something that makes a team's own life easier, not a top-down compliance requirement.

## Checklist: quarterly or annual decision log audit

* [ ] Skim for records stuck in `request for comments` or `proposed` with no resolution — close them out or explicitly archive them.
* [ ] Check that accepted decisions with a stated review cadence were actually reviewed.
* [ ] Check for decisions that were informally reversed in practice but never marked `superseded` in the log — update them.
* [ ] Spot-check a few older records: would a new hire today understand the context without asking someone?
* [ ] Confirm the log is still stored somewhere the current team actually uses — tooling and habits drift over a year.
