# Enter the mid-market segment

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-03-10
* Decision-maker(s): CEO, with Board approval
* Owner: VP Strategy

Summary: We will extend our enterprise-only go-to-market to include the mid-market segment (100–1,000 employees), starting Q3, funded by reallocating 15% of the enterprise sales team's capacity.

## Issue

Our enterprise pipeline has plateaued at roughly 40 qualified accounts per quarter in our addressable territories, while win rate has stayed flat for two years. Meanwhile, inbound interest from smaller companies (100–1,000 employees) has grown 3x year over year, and we currently turn this demand away or serve it poorly with an enterprise-configured sales motion. The board has asked for a credible growth path beyond enterprise account saturation.

## Assumptions

* Mid-market buyers convert faster (assumed 45-day average cycle vs. 120 days enterprise) but at roughly one-third the average contract value.
* Our product requires moderate simplification (fewer configuration steps) to be viable for mid-market self-serve onboarding.
* Existing enterprise sales talent is not optimized for a higher-velocity, lower-touch motion and would need retraining or augmentation.

## Constraints

* No net-new headcount approved for FY26; any mid-market motion must be funded from existing budget.
* Enterprise revenue targets for the current fiscal year are not to be put at risk.
* Legal has flagged that our current MSA terms assume enterprise-scale procurement cycles and will need a simplified version for faster-closing deals.

## Options considered

* **Stay enterprise-only, invest further in enterprise expansion.** Lowest execution risk, but doesn't address the plateau; several board members are skeptical this alone reaches next year's growth target.
* **Enter mid-market via a dedicated new team, funded by new headcount.** Cleanest execution, but blocked by the current-year hiring freeze.
* **Enter mid-market by reallocating 15% of existing enterprise sales capacity, with a simplified onboarding motion.** Slower ramp than a dedicated team, but fundable within current headcount and reversible within two quarters if it underperforms.
* **Enter mid-market via a channel/reseller partnership instead of direct sales.** Lowest internal cost, but reference calls with two peer companies indicated partner-led motions underperform direct sales by roughly 2x in the first year, and we'd cede the customer relationship.

## Argument

The reallocation option is the only one that is both fundable under this year's constraints and reversible if the underlying assumption about mid-market conversion speed proves wrong. It also gives us real data on mid-market economics before committing to a dedicated team investment in FY27 budget planning, rather than committing headcount to an unproven motion.

## Decision

We will enter the mid-market segment starting Q3, staffed by reallocating 15% of enterprise sales capacity into a mid-market pod, with a simplified onboarding flow and adjusted MSA terms for the segment.

## Implications

* VP Sales will identify which 15% of enterprise capacity moves, by end of Q2.
* Product will scope the onboarding simplification for a Q3 release.
* Legal will draft a simplified MSA variant for mid-market accounts.
* VP Strategy will report mid-market conversion and CAC data to the board at the end of Q3 and Q4, as the basis for a FY27 dedicated-team investment decision.
* Enterprise revenue targets remain unchanged; if the reallocation measurably harms enterprise pipeline, this decision will be revisited before FY27 planning.
