# Choose a hybrid working model

* Status: request for comments
* Date: 2026-08-20
* Decision-maker(s): Executive team (to be confirmed)
* Owner: Chief People Officer

Summary: Work in progress. This is a starting point for a team that is deciding how a hybrid working model should work in practice: how often, which days, how space is used, and how fairness is kept across roles. It shows how a team can frame the question, debate options, and gather information. It records no decision.

## Issue

Staff have worked mostly from home for some time without a written policy, and different managers now expect different things. An engagement survey suggests that many staff want to work remotely much of the week, while several managers report that in-person days help onboarding and work across teams. The lease on the main office will end, so the team must also decide how much space to keep.

The questions to answer:

* What do staff, managers, and customers need, and where do they conflict?
* What in-person time is useful, for which kinds of work?
* What model is clear, fair across roles, and affordable?
* How much space do we need, and what flexibility should the lease allow?

## Assumptions

Each assumption is something to test.

* **Survey responses reflect what staff will actually do.** Test: compare stated preferences with badge and booking data, where available.
* **In-person days help onboarding and cross-team work.** Test: ask new hires and team leaders what helped; compare teams with different patterns.
* **Teams can agree days that suit their work.** Test: pilot with a few teams and see how they decide.
* **A fraction of staff in the office on a typical day is enough capacity.** Test: use occupancy data and booking patterns, including peak days.

## Constraints

* The lease decision has a deadline.
* Roles that cannot be done remotely need a fair approach.
* Some staff will need different arrangements, for example because of a disability or caring duties.
* Unknown, to be confirmed: costs of space and travel, legal requirements, and local labour market expectations.

## Options considered

For each option, estimate value, costs, timeline, and capability needs, and note what evidence would change the team's mind.

* **Office-first.** Everyone is in the office most days.
  * Value: simple; supports in-person learning.
  * Risks: conflicts with what many staff say they want; narrows the hiring pool.
* **Remote-first.** No required office days, with space for meetings.
  * Value: lowest property cost; widest hiring pool.
  * Risks: harder onboarding and cross-team work; roles that cannot be done remotely are left in a separate category.
* **Team-set anchor days.** Each team chooses a few shared in-person days a week within company limits.
  * Value: fits different kinds of work; keeps benefits of being together.
  * Cost drivers: space planning; booking systems; manager time to apply rules consistently.
* **Company-set days for everyone.** Simple and easy to plan space for, but less flexible.
* **Pilot, then decide.** Try two models in different parts of the business and compare.

## Argument

Points of debate the team should expect:

* **Simplicity versus fit.** One rule is easy to explain; different rules fit different work.
* **Evidence.** Research suggests hybrid arrangements are often a workable middle (for example work by Nicholas Bloom and colleagues), but results depend on the type of work and on management practice.
* **Fairness.** How do we treat roles that cannot be done remotely?
* **Space.** Fewer desks reduce cost but require booking and discipline.

How the team could evaluate costs and trade-offs:

1. Choose outcome measures: onboarding speed, collaboration, retention, performance, wellbeing, and cost.
2. Estimate costs for each option: space (ranging from several scenarios of attendance), facilities, travel, equipment, management time, and the cost of recruiting if people leave. Show ranges.
3. Consider the effect on health and psychological wellbeing, including isolation and commuting.
4. Compare options under best, likely, and worst scenarios.
5. Plan regular reviews of the measures, with named owners and rules for adjusting.

## Decision

**Not yet decided.** This is a starting point. A team using it should replace this section with its own decision.

Suggested way to reach a decision:

* Confirm who decides and who is consulted.
* Agree criteria and weights first.
* Gather the evidence above and record what was found.
* Share for comment, then decide and update the Status.

## Implications

If the team decides on team-set anchor days, it would need to:

* Publish the policy and a template for each team's written agreement.
* Decide how to handle roles that cannot be done remotely.
* Introduce desk and room booking.
* Hold managers accountable for agreeing days and applying rules equally.
* Review the pattern of each team after a stated period.
