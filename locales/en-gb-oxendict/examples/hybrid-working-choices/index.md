# Choose a hybrid working model

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-08-20
* Decision-maker(s): Executive team
* Owner: Chief People Officer

## Context

Since 2021 our staff have worked mostly from home without a written policy, and different managers now expect different things. An engagement survey (response rate 81%) shows that 62% of staff want to work remotely at least three days a week, while several managers report that in-person days help onboarding and cross-team work. Our lease on the main office ends in 14 months and we have to decide how much space to keep. We need a hybrid working model that is clear, fair across roles, and affordable.

## Options considered

* **Office-first.** Everyone is in the office at least four days a week. Simple to explain and supports in-person learning, but it conflicts with what most staff say they want and narrows the talent we can hire from.
* **Remote-first.** No required office days, with a small space for meetings. Lowest property cost and the widest hiring pool, but it makes onboarding and cross-team work harder and leaves roles that cannot be done remotely in a separate category.
* **Team-set anchor days.** Each team chooses two or three shared in-person days a week that suit its work, inside company limits on minimum and maximum office time. Fits different kinds of work and keeps the benefits of being together, but it makes office capacity harder to plan and needs managers to apply the rules consistently.

## Decision

We will adopt team-set anchor days: each team agrees in writing two to three shared in-person days a week, no team is required to be in the office more than three days, and the in-person days of each team are reviewed every six months. We will renew the lease for space that seats about 55% of staff on a typical day.

## Consequences

* The Chief People Officer publishes the policy, a template for a team's written agreement, and the rules on how to handle roles that cannot be done remotely.
* Managers are accountable for agreeing the anchor days with their teams and for applying the rules equally, and we will check this in the next engagement survey.
* Because only about 55% of staff are seated at a time, desks are booked rather than assigned; we accept that some staff will lose a fixed desk.
* Requests for different arrangements, for example because of a disability, follow [Set a process for workplace reasonable adjustments](../workplace-reasonable-adjustments/) rather than the team agreement.
* We give up the simplicity of one rule for everyone, in exchange for matching the arrangement to the work.

## Related

* This decision does not change pay or location-based allowances. Whether to link pay to work location is a separate decision.
