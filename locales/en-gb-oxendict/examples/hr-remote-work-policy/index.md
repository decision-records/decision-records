# Adopt a hybrid remote-work policy

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-01-08
* Decision-maker(s): CEO and Head of People, with executive team input
* Owner: Head of People

Summary: We will move from a fully-remote policy to a hybrid policy requiring two anchor days per week in-office for employees within commuting distance of a company office, effective next quarter.

## Issue

Employee survey data over the last year shows a split: employees within commuting distance report reduced cross-team collaboration and slower onboarding for new hires, while fully-remote employees (outside commuting distance) report high satisfaction and cite remote flexibility as a top reason for joining. Leadership must decide whether to standardize a single policy or maintain a split arrangement, and if hybrid, how many anchor days to require.

## Assumptions

* In-office collaboration meaningfully improves onboarding speed and cross-team coordination for employees who can access an office, based on onboarding-time data comparing our two prior office cohorts.
* A policy requiring in-office days only for those within commuting distance is administrable without appearing arbitrary or unfair, if clearly documented.
* Real estate footprint is sufficient to support two-anchor-day attendance without added office investment.

## Constraints

* Cannot require relocation or in-office attendance for employees hired explicitly as fully-remote, per their offer terms.
* Any policy change must be communicated with at least one full quarter's notice.
* Legal has confirmed the policy must be applied consistently within each employee category to avoid discrimination exposure.

## Options considered

* **Keep fully remote for everyone.** Preserves current employee satisfaction scores and avoids a difficult policy change, but does not address the documented onboarding and collaboration gaps raised independently by three department heads.
* **Require full-time in-office for all commuting-distance employees.** Directly addresses collaboration and onboarding concerns, but survey data suggests this would trigger meaningful regretted attrition among employees who joined explicitly for remote flexibility.
* **Hybrid: two anchor days per week for commuting-distance employees; fully remote unchanged for those outside commuting distance.** Targets the specific population where the data shows a real gap, without changing terms for employees hired as fully remote.

## Argument

The onboarding and collaboration data specifically implicates commuting-distance employees who are currently choosing not to come in, not remote employees generally. A blanket policy change would create attrition risk not supported by the data; a targeted hybrid policy addresses the actual gap while explicitly preserving the terms remote-hired employees joined under.

## Decision

We will adopt a hybrid policy requiring two anchor days per week in-office for employees within commuting distance of a company office, effective next quarter. Employees hired explicitly as fully remote are unaffected.

## Implications

* Head of People will communicate the policy with a full quarter's notice, including which specific days are anchor days per office.
* Facilities will confirm office capacity supports two-day attendance without added real estate investment.
* Managers will need guidance on applying the policy consistently within their teams.
* People Operations will track onboarding-time and collaboration survey metrics for two quarters post-rollout to confirm the policy achieves its intended effect.
* This decision does not apply to future fully-remote offers unless a separate decision changes that hiring category going forward.
