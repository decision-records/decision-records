# Set a process for workplace reasonable adjustments

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-03-30
* Decision-maker(s): Chief People Officer
* Owner: Head of Employee Experience

## Context

In many places the law requires an employer to make reasonable adjustments so that a disabled employee or candidate is not at a disadvantage, and it is also simply the right way to treat people. Our requests are currently handled differently by each manager: some people wait weeks, some are asked to explain their condition repeatedly, and some are refused with no reason given. Managers also tell us they do not know what is reasonable or who pays. We need a clear process that is fast, private, and fair.

## Options considered

* **Leave it to line managers.** Keeps decisions close to the person and the job, but it produces inconsistent results, asks managers to make decisions they are not trained for, and exposes medical details to more people than needed.
* **Send every request to a central team.** Consistent and private, but it can be slow for simple requests such as a different chair or software, and the central team may not understand the role.
* **A central team sets the process and pays, and managers make the decision on advice.** The person describes what they need, not their diagnosis; the manager and the person agree an adjustment, using advice from a trained adviser where needed, with a central budget so cost does not decide the outcome. Fast for simple requests and consistent, but it needs training, an adviser, and a central budget.

## Decision

We will adopt the third option: a central budget, a trained adviser, and decisions made between the person and their manager on the basis of what the person needs and not their diagnosis. Simple requests will be agreed within five working days, and any refusal must be given in writing with the reason and a route to appeal.

## Consequences

* The Head of Employee Experience will publish the process, the form, the expected timelines, and a guide for managers.
* Managers will be trained before they handle requests, and will not be told a diagnosis unless the person chooses to share it.
* A central budget pays for adjustments, so that team budgets do not decide outcomes; we will report the number, timing, and cost of requests each year without identifying individuals.
* Adjustments will be reviewed when the person's role or health changes, and not only when a problem arises.
* We accept the cost of the budget, the adviser, and manager training, in exchange for fairness, speed, and lower legal risk.
