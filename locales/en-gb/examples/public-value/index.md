# Weigh public value in a service redesign

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-06-18
* Decision-maker(s): Director General, with the departmental board
* Owner: Director of Service Design

## Context

Our agency processes about 400,000 benefit claims a year. Claims take a median of 38 days, and a recent review found that the people who wait longest are those least able to complete the online form: older people, people with limited English, and people without reliable internet access. A proposal would move all claims to a digital-only service and close two of the four local offices, saving an estimated 14% of operating cost. Because this is a public service, a choice that is efficient is not enough: it also has to be legitimate, fair, and something we can sustain, which are the three parts of the public value strategic triangle (legitimacy and support, public value created, and operational capacity).

## Options considered

* **Digital-only service, with two local offices closed.** Delivers the largest saving and the fastest median processing time for those who use it, but it shifts the burden onto the people who can least bear it and is likely to meet strong opposition from local representatives, which weakens legitimacy and support.
* **Digital-first service, with assisted routes kept.** Digital is the default, but the four offices stay open as assisted-digital centres, and a telephone route stays available. Gives most of the processing benefit while protecting access, but the saving is much smaller, about 5%.
* **Keep the current mix and improve it.** Make only process improvements. Lowest risk and no access concerns, but it leaves the 38-day median largely unchanged and does not use the opportunity digital tools offer.

## Decision

We will build a digital-first service and keep the four offices as assisted-digital centres with a telephone route. We will judge the redesign on public value, not saving alone: median processing time, the gap in processing time between the slowest-served and the fastest-served groups, the number of successful first-time claims, and the saving.

## Consequences

* The Director of Service Design will publish the measures and report them quarterly, including the gap between the groups of claimants, to the departmental board and to the public.
* We accept a smaller saving (about 5% instead of 14%) in exchange for fair access and for legitimacy with local representatives and claimants.
* We will co-design the assisted-digital service with claimants who currently wait longest, and publish what we learned.
* We will revisit the office network only after the first year of data on who uses which route, and any proposal to close an office will be a new decision.
* Operational capacity: the assisted routes need trained staff, so training is funded before launch and not as an afterthought.
