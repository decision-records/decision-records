# Hybrid remote-work policy

* Status: request for comments
* Date: 2026-01-08
* Decision-maker(s): CEO and Head of People, with executive team input (to be confirmed)
* Owner: Head of People

Summary: Work in progress. This is a starting point for a team that is considering moving from a fully remote policy to a hybrid policy for people who live within reach of an office. It shows how a team can frame the question, debate the options, and gather information. It records no decision.

## Issue

An organization has worked fully remotely for some years. Survey results suggest a split: people who live within commuting distance of an office report less collaboration across teams and slower onboarding for new hires, while people who live far from any office report high satisfaction and cite remote flexibility as a main reason for joining. Leadership must decide whether to keep one policy for everyone, move to a single standard, or keep a split arrangement.

The questions to answer:

* What is the evidence that location affects collaboration, onboarding, and performance here?
* Which groups would be affected, and how, by each option?
* How would any in-office expectation be set, explained, and applied fairly?
* What would we measure, and for how long, to know whether the policy works?

## Assumptions

Each assumption is something to test.

* **In-person time improves onboarding and cross-team work.** Test: compare onboarding times and collaboration measures for people with different amounts of in-person time, and ask new hires what helped. Treat correlations with care.
* **A policy that applies only to people within commuting distance can be explained as fair.** Test: draft the policy and ask a mixed group to say how it feels.
* **Existing offices can host the expected attendance.** Test: check capacity, desks, meeting rooms, and the cost of extending.
* **Remote flexibility matters to people who joined for it.** Test: look at attrition risk by group, and ask directly.

## Constraints

* People hired explicitly as fully remote cannot be required to relocate or attend, under their terms.
* Any change needs sufficient notice to staff.
* Rules must be applied consistently within each group of employees to avoid discrimination; take legal advice for your jurisdiction.
* Unknown, to be confirmed: the costs of space, travel, and any allowances, and requests for adjustments from people with disabilities or caring responsibilities.

## Options considered

For each option, estimate value, costs, timeline, and capability needs, and note what evidence would change the team's mind.

* **Keep fully remote for everyone.**
  * Value: keeps current satisfaction; no disruption.
  * Risks: does not address the reported gaps in onboarding and collaboration.
* **Require full-time office attendance for people within commuting distance.**
  * Value: directly addresses collaboration and onboarding concerns.
  * Cost drivers: office space and facilities; commuting burden; likely loss of people who joined for flexibility.
* **Hybrid: a number of anchor days each week in the office for people within commuting distance; remote unchanged for others.**
  * Value: targets the group where the gap is reported, without changing terms for people hired as remote.
  * Cost drivers: space planning; communication; manager time to apply rules consistently.
* **Team-set rhythms.** Teams choose their own days together, within company limits.
* **Pilot first**, trying the hybrid model in some locations or teams and comparing results before a wider policy.

## Argument

Points of debate the team should expect:

* **Evidence quality.** Research on remote and hybrid work exists (for example randomized studies of working from home and surveys of hybrid arrangements), but results depend on the type of work. What applies here?
* **Fairness.** Different rules for different groups can feel arbitrary unless the reasons are clear.
* **Autonomy versus coordination.** Trust and flexibility versus the benefits of being together.
* **Risk of losing people.** Who is most likely to leave, and how costly would that be?

How the team could evaluate costs and trade-offs:

1. Describe the outcomes wanted (onboarding speed, collaboration, retention, performance, wellbeing) and how each will be measured.
2. Cost each option: space, facilities, travel, management time, communications, and the cost of lost or hard-to-hire people. Show ranges.
3. Consider health and safety and psychological wellbeing, including commuting and isolation.
4. Compare options under best, likely, and worst scenarios.
5. Plan a review after a stated period, with named measures and a rule for what to do depending on what they show.

## Decision

**Not yet decided.** This is a starting point. A team using it should replace this section with its own decision.

Suggested way to reach a decision:

* Confirm who decides and who is consulted.
* Agree criteria and weights first.
* Gather the evidence above and record what was found.
* Share for comment, then decide and update the Status.

## Implications

If the team decides on a hybrid policy, it would need to:

* Give staff a full period of notice and say which days apply in each office.
* Confirm that office capacity supports the expected attendance.
* Give managers guidance on applying the policy consistently.
* Track onboarding and collaboration measures for a stated period.
* Decide how requests for individual adjustments will be handled.
