# Reposition the brand around reliability

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-01-15
* Decision-maker(s): CMO
* Owner: Head of Brand

Summary: We will reposition our core brand message from "innovative" to "reliable," reflecting where customer research shows we actually win and lose deals, with a phased rollout starting with sales collateral and paid channels.

## Issue

Win/loss interviews over the last two quarters consistently show customers choosing us over faster-moving competitors specifically because of our uptime track record and support responsiveness — not because they perceive us as more innovative, which is our current brand positioning. Our messaging is actively working against the reason customers say they buy from us.

## Assumptions

* The reliability advantage is durable, not a temporary artifact of a competitor's recent outage.
* Existing brand equity built around "innovative" over the last three years is not so entrenched that repositioning will confuse existing customers.
* Sales and Customer Success can articulate the new positioning consistently once trained.

## Constraints

* No budget for a full rebrand (new logo, visual identity); this is a messaging repositioning within the existing visual system.
* Must not contradict claims already made in signed enterprise contracts referencing current messaging.
* Legal requires any reliability claims (e.g., uptime statistics) to be independently verifiable before publication.

## Options considered

* **Keep "innovative" positioning, invest in proof points to make it more credible.** Avoids repositioning risk, but doesn't address that win/loss data shows this isn't why customers actually choose us — this option optimizes the wrong message.
* **Reposition fully around "reliable," phased rollout over two quarters.** Aligns messaging with actual purchase drivers; phased approach limits risk if the shift underperforms.
* **Run both messages in parallel, segmented by channel.** Avoids committing fully, but brand consistency research suggests mixed messaging measurably weakens recall; two prior internal experiments with segmented messaging underperformed a single consistent message.

## Argument

Win/loss data is the strongest signal we have of what customers actually value, and it points clearly at reliability. A phased rollout (collateral and paid channels first, full brand refresh deferred) lets us validate the new message's performance before committing budget we don't currently have to a full visual rebrand.

## Decision

We will reposition core brand messaging around reliability, rolling out first through sales collateral and paid channels, with a full brand-refresh decision deferred pending performance data.

## Implications

* Head of Brand will update sales collateral and paid channel creative within one quarter.
* Legal will validate all uptime and reliability claims before any new asset ships.
* Sales and Customer Success will receive updated talking points and messaging training.
* A follow-on decision record will assess full visual rebrand investment once phase-one performance data is available, expected in two quarters.
* Existing signed contracts referencing "innovative" positioning are unaffected; this is a forward-looking messaging change only.
