# Written ways of working

* Status: request for comments
* Date: 2026-04-27
* Decision-maker(s): Head of Engineering (to be confirmed)
* Owner: Engineering Manager

Summary: Work in progress. This is a starting point for a team that is considering whether to write down shared ways of working (principles, ground rules, and team agreements) as a group grows. It shows how a team can frame the question, debate options, and gather information. It records no decision.

## Issue

A product and engineering group has grown quickly, and expectations that used to be understood are now different between teams: how quickly to answer a message, when a decision needs a meeting, how to disagree, and what counts as done. New joiners report learning these by making mistakes. The group needs ways of working that are explicit, understood, open to debate, and quick to flag when someone breaks them.

The questions to answer:

* Which expectations differ most, and which of those cause real problems?
* What should be common to the whole group, and what should each team decide?
* How should agreements be written, kept, found, and changed?
* How will we know whether they help?

## Assumptions

Each assumption is something to test.

* **Most friction comes from unstated expectations**, not from disagreement about them. Test: ask people to describe how decisions get made and compare the answers.
* **Teams keep agreements they wrote themselves.** Test: pilot with a team and review after a while.
* **A short shared set is enough.** Test: draft a few principles and ask people if they would read and use them.
* **People feel safe to raise problems.** Test: ask; psychological safety, as studied by Amy Edmondson and others, is a condition for any agreement to work.

## Constraints

* The group-wide set must be short enough to read in a few minutes.
* Teams have limited time to write and review agreements.
* Agreements must be kept where new joiners find them on their first day.
* Unknown, to be confirmed: the differing needs of design, engineering, and support.

## Options considered

For each option, estimate value, costs, timeline, and capability needs, and note what evidence would change the team's mind.

* **Rely on culture and onboarding conversations.**
  * Value: no effort.
  * Risks: already failing for people who join later.
* **Adopt one company-wide set of ways of working.**
  * Value: consistency.
  * Risks: written by a few, may feel imposed; may not fit very different teams.
* **Write a short group-wide set and let each team add its own working agreement.**
  * Value: consistency and ownership.
  * Cost drivers: team time to write and review; a place to keep and find agreements; someone to maintain the shared set.
* **Start with one or two teams**, learn what works, then extend.
* **Use a published model** (for example Team Topologies for team interaction modes) as a starting vocabulary, adapted to local needs.

## Argument

Points of debate the team should expect:

* **Rules or principles?** Principles invite judgement; rules are easier to check.
* **Who writes?** A few leaders, or everyone? Participation builds ownership but takes time.
* **How binding?** What happens when someone breaks an agreement, and who raises it?
* **Change.** If anyone can propose changes, how are they decided?

How the team could evaluate costs and trade-offs:

1. Estimate the cost of the current approach: time lost to misunderstanding, slow onboarding, repeated discussions.
2. Estimate the cost of writing and maintaining agreements: facilitation, review meetings, tools.
3. Choose measures: onboarding feedback, number of repeated disputes, results of short pulse surveys, and whether people can say where agreements are and how to change them.
4. Compare the options on value, cost, and speed; decide how and when to review.
5. Try a pilot and define in advance what a good result would be.

## Decision

**Not yet decided.** This is a starting point. A team using it should replace this section with its own decision.

Suggested way to reach a decision:

* Confirm who decides and who is consulted.
* Agree criteria first.
* Gather the evidence above and record what was found.
* Share for comment, then decide and update the Status.

## Implications

If the team decides on a short shared set plus team agreements, it would need to:

* Name who drafts the shared set, who reviews, and when it is published.
* Ask each team to write its agreement soon after publication and to keep it where new joiners find it.
* Make each agreement name who handles a breakage.
* Review agreements regularly and check twice a year that people know them.
