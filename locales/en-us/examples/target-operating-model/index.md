# Adopt a target operating model

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-09-15
* Decision-maker(s): CEO and executive team
* Owner: Chief Operating Officer

## Context

Over six years we grew from 60 to 450 people by acquisition and by hiring, and our organization now reflects that history rather than a plan: three business units run their own finance, support, and procurement processes, and customers meet different teams, systems, and prices depending on which unit they first contacted. Leaders cannot say who is accountable for end-to-end outcomes such as onboarding a customer. We need a target operating model (TOM) that describes how the organization will deliver its strategy: its processes, people, technology, locations, and governance.

## Options considered

* **Keep the business-unit model and harmonize at the edges.** Lowest disruption and keeps local autonomy, but it leaves duplicated functions in place (about 40 roles overlap) and does not give customers a single experience.
* **Move to a functional model.** Sales, delivery, finance, and support each become one company-wide function. Removes duplication and gives clear functional standards, but it separates teams from the customers and products they serve, and our two largest units told us delivery speed would suffer.
* **Move to a hybrid model: shared services plus customer-aligned teams.** Finance, people, procurement, and support become shared services; sales and delivery stay aligned to customer segments. Captures most of the efficiency of the functional model while keeping delivery close to customers, but it needs clear service levels between the shared services and the teams that depend on them, and it is the most complex to design.

## Decision

We will adopt the hybrid model as our target operating model: shared services for finance, people, procurement, and support, and customer-aligned teams for sales and delivery. We will describe the target in one document covering processes, roles, technology, locations, and governance, and we will move toward it in stages, starting with the shared services that have the clearest duplication.

## Consequences

* The Chief Operating Officer owns the target operating model document and publishes the first version within eight weeks.
* Each shared service will publish service levels and a named owner before any team is moved to it, so the teams that depend on it know what to expect.
* Roles in the overlapping functions will change; a separate decision will record how we handle those changes, and no one will be told of an individual outcome before it is decided.
* Business-unit leaders give up some autonomy over support functions, in exchange for one customer experience and lower duplicated cost.
* We will review the model after four quarters against the measures set in the document: onboarding time, cost of shared services, and customer satisfaction by segment.
