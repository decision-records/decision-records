# Phase in a business framework

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-10-01
* Decision-maker(s): CEO and executive team
* Owner: Chief of Staff

## Context

Leadership wants to adopt Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) as our company-wide goal-setting framework, because teams currently set goals in different formats and cadences and leaders cannot see how team work connects to company priorities. Our last attempt to roll out a new planning process to all 240 employees at once stalled within two quarters: managers were not trained, the tooling was unsettled, and people treated it as extra reporting. We need to choose how to introduce the framework, not whether to.

## Options considered

* **Big-bang rollout.** Every team adopts OKRs at the start of the next quarter. Gets one shared language fastest and avoids running two systems side by side, but repeats the conditions that caused the last rollout to stall: no time to train managers, no proven examples, and no chance to fix the framework before everyone depends on it.
* **Phased rollout over three quarters.** Start with the executive team, add a small group of pilot departments, then extend company-wide. Costs about two additional quarters before every team is aligned, and for that time two goal-setting systems coexist, but each phase teaches us what to change before the next one.
* **Adopt only at the executive level.** Lowest effort and lowest risk, but it does not solve the visibility problem, since team goals would still be in different formats and cadences.

## Decision

We will phase in OKRs over three quarters: the executive team in the first quarter, three pilot departments (Sales, Product, and Customer Support) in the second, and all remaining teams in the third. Each phase begins only when the exit criteria for the previous phase are met: leaders have been trained, goals have been scored at the end of a full cycle, and the pilot teams report that the process is worth the time it takes.

## Consequences

* The Chief of Staff will run the rollout, publish the exit criteria for each phase, and report progress to the executive team monthly.
* Until the third quarter, most teams keep their current goal-setting format, so company-wide visibility will be incomplete during the rollout. We accept this in exchange for fixing the framework before everyone depends on it.
* Pilot departments take on extra training and review time, which we will budget for explicitly rather than absorb silently.
* We will decide the tooling (a shared document or a dedicated product) at the end of the second quarter, using what the pilot teams actually needed, not before.
* If a phase's exit criteria are not met, we repeat that phase for another quarter instead of advancing; a follow-on decision will record any change to the plan.

## Related

* This decision does not change compensation or performance reviews; whether to link them to OKRs is a separate decision that we will not take until after the third phase.
