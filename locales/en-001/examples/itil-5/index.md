# Phase in ITIL 5

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-09-30
* Decision-maker(s): Chief Information Officer
* Owner: Head of IT Service Management

## Context

Our IT service desk supports 3,200 staff and has grown without a common way of working: incidents, requests, and changes are handled in different ways by three teams, and recurring incidents are seldom analysed. The CIO has chosen to base our IT service management on ITIL 5, the latest ITIL guidance, so that we share common practices and language with our suppliers and auditors. Adopting it at once across all teams would stop the service desk's own improvement work for months. We need to decide how to phase it in. This record describes our adoption plan and does not restate what ITIL 5 says.

## Options considered

* **All at once.** All teams adopt the chosen practices at the same time, so we have one way of working soonest, but the service desk has no capacity to retrain and to run the service in the same quarter, and we would be unable to adjust the practices to our context.
* **Phase in by practice.** Start with the practices that cause most pain and affect most users (incident and request handling), then change, then problem analysis and continual improvement, each in a separate phase with its own measures. Each phase lasts about a quarter and teaches us what to change next, but full adoption takes about a year and the old and new ways of working coexist for a time.
* **Phase in by team.** One team adopts all the practices first as a pilot, then the others follow. Gives a complete working example early, but it splits one service across two ways of working while the pilot runs, and that confuses users and suppliers.

## Decision

We will phase in ITIL 5 by practice over four quarters: incident and request handling first, then change, then problem analysis, then continual improvement. A phase ends only when the previous phase's measures have been met for a full month: resolution time, first-time fix, and the share of changes that cause incidents.

## Consequences

* The Head of IT Service Management will lead the plan, publish the exit measures for each phase, and report to the CIO monthly.
* Staff will be trained in each practice just before its phase and not all at the start.
* For about a year, practices that have not yet been phased in will keep working in the current way, which we accept.
* Tooling changes are limited to what a phase requires; we will not buy a new service management platform until the end of the second phase and then decide with evidence.
* If a phase misses its measures, we repeat it for one more quarter and record the reason.
