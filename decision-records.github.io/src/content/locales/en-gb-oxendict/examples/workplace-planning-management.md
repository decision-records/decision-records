# Adopt workplace planning and management

* Status: request for comments
* Date: 2026-10-08
* Decision-maker(s): Chief Operating Officer (to be confirmed)
* Owner: Head of Workplace

Summary: Work in progress. This is a starting point for a team that is considering how to plan and manage its workplace as one system, using evidence of how space is used. It shows how a team can frame the question, debate options, and gather information. It records no decision.

## Issue

After the organization chose team-set anchor days for hybrid working, office use became much harder to predict: some days are busy and others nearly empty, and teams that share a floor have found no room to meet on the same day. Space, desks, meeting rooms, cleaning, and security are managed by separate groups with separate data. The organization needs a way to plan and manage the workplace as one system, so that space fits the work and the property budget is well spent.

The questions to answer:

* How is space really used (by day, floor, and type of space), and what do people need?
* What data can be collected without being intrusive or unlawful, and how will staff be told?
* Who owns the whole picture?
* How do we adjust space over time, including the lease?

## Assumptions

Each assumption is something to test.

* **Aggregated data shows how space is used without tracking individuals.** Test: define the data, check that it cannot be traced to individuals, and review with privacy specialists.
* **Teams will book their zones for the days of their agreements.** Test: pilot with a few teams.
* **Fitting desks and rooms to demand reduces waste.** Test: compare occupancy with capacity over several weeks and different seasons.
* **Staff will accept shared desks if booking works well.** Test: ask, and run a pilot.

## Constraints

* Usage should be reported only in aggregate (by floor and by day), never about individuals.
* Staff must be told how data is used before it is collected; privacy law applies (for example the GDPR in Europe).
* Adjustments for individuals are handled through a separate process and must not be hindered by booking rules.
* Unknown, to be confirmed: lease break points, existing systems, and accessibility needs.

## Options considered

For each option, estimate value, costs, timeline, and capability needs, and note what evidence would change the team's mind.

* **Keep managing space by group.**
  * Value: no change in effort.
  * Risks: decisions continue to conflict; no overall picture.
* **Buy an integrated workplace management platform and let it assign desks.**
  * Value: complete data and automation.
  * Cost drivers: licences; integration; configuration; change management.
  * Risks: automatic assignment may ignore what teams need to be together.
* **Plan from usage data, with team bookings.** One owner for space, bookings, and services; badge, booking, and sensor-free data; a regular plan of space by day; bookable team zones.
  * Value: matches space to demand and keeps teams together.
  * Cost drivers: data work; booking tool; the owner's time.
* **Survey-based approach** using regular staff surveys and observation instead of automated data, at lower privacy risk but less precision.
* **Pilot on one floor** before extending.

## Argument

Points of debate the team should expect:

* **Data versus privacy.** What is the minimum data needed, and what would staff accept?
* **Choice versus coordination.** Fixed desks give stability; shared desks give efficiency.
* **Ownership.** Combining groups into one function has people effects.
* **Lease.** Reducing space saves cost, but removing it is hard to undo.

How the team could evaluate costs and trade-offs:

1. Measure occupancy and booking patterns over a meaningful period and compare with capacity.
2. Cost the options: space (existing and scenarios), systems, booking tools, staff time, change effort, and moves. Show ranges.
3. Consider benefits: space savings, better team co-location, lower service costs, better experience.
4. Assess privacy risk and mitigations, with specialist input; consider ISO/IEC 27701 and privacy-by-design thinking.
5. Review after the first year of data, using named measures.

## Decision

**Not yet decided.** This is a starting point. A team using it should replace this section with its own decision.

Suggested way to reach a decision:

* Confirm who decides and who is consulted (including privacy and employee representatives).
* Agree criteria and weights first.
* Gather the evidence above and record what was found.
* Share for comment, then decide and update the Status.

## Implications

If the team decides on usage-based planning with bookable team zones, it would need to:

* Name an owner for space, bookings, cleaning, and security services.
* Publish a regular plan of space by day.
* Tell staff how data is collected and used before collecting it.
* Fit desks and rooms to measured demand, which may mean fewer fixed desks and more meeting space.
* Review the lease at the next break point using a year of data.
