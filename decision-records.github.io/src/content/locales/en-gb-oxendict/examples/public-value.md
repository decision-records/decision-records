# Weigh public value in a service redesign

* Status: request for comments
* Date: 2026-06-18
* Decision-maker(s): Director General, with the departmental board (to be confirmed)
* Owner: Director of Service Design

Summary: Work in progress. This is a starting point for a public body that is redesigning a service and wants to weigh public value, not only efficiency. It shows how a team can frame the question, debate options, and gather information. It records no decision.

## Issue

A public agency processes a large number of benefit claims. Claims take a long time, and a recent review found that the people who wait longest are those least able to complete the online form: older people, people with limited language skills, and people without reliable internet access. A proposal would move all claims to a digital-only service and close some local offices to save operating cost.

Because this is a public service, an efficient choice is not enough. It must also be legitimate, fair, and something the agency can sustain. Mark Moore's public value strategic triangle names these three parts: legitimacy and support, public value created, and operational capacity.

The questions to answer:

* Who uses the service, and who is left behind by each option?
* What does the public value, and how will we know we deliver it?
* What do elected representatives, claimants, staff, and advocates think, and why?
* What can the agency actually deliver and sustain?

## Assumptions

Each assumption is something to test.

* **The people who wait longest are those least able to use a digital-only service.** Test: analyse claims by route, time, and characteristics; interview claimants.
* **Assisted digital support can bring most claimants to the digital route over time.** Test: pilot in one or two offices and measure outcomes.
* **Opposition to closures would weaken support.** Test: consult local representatives and community groups early.
* **Staff can be trained for assisted work.** Test: ask staff and managers, and estimate the training time.

## Constraints

* The service must be legitimate and fair, not only efficient.
* Trained staff must be funded before launch.
* Any proposal to close an office should be a separate decision.
* Unknown, to be confirmed: legal duties of access and equality, the budget for the redesign, and the effect of other reforms.

## Options considered

For each option, estimate value, costs, timeline, and capability needs, and note what evidence would change the team's mind.

* **Digital-only service, with some offices closed.**
  * Value: largest saving; fastest processing for those who can use it.
  * Risks: shifts the burden to those who can least bear it; likely to meet strong opposition, weakening legitimacy.
* **Digital-first service, with assisted routes kept.** Digital is the default, local offices remain as assisted-digital centres, and a telephone route stays.
  * Value: most of the processing benefit while protecting access.
  * Cost drivers: staff training; running multiple channels; smaller saving.
* **Keep the current mix and improve it.** Process improvements only.
  * Value: lowest risk; no access concerns.
  * Risks: leaves long waits largely unchanged and does not use digital opportunities.
* **Co-design with claimants** before choosing a model, and test several service patterns in pilots.

## Argument

Points of debate the team should expect:

* **Efficiency versus equity.** How much saving is worth how much less access? Who decides?
* **Whose voice counts?** Claimants, staff, representatives, and advocates may disagree.
* **Measures.** Median processing time alone can hide unfairness. The gap between the slowest-served and fastest-served groups matters.
* **Sustainability.** Can the agency maintain assisted services over time?

How the team could evaluate costs and trade-offs:

1. Use cost-benefit thinking that includes social costs and benefits, for example as set out in public-sector appraisal guidance such as the UK Treasury's Green Book. Count benefits to claimants (time, stress, access) as well as costs to the agency.
2. Estimate operating costs and savings of each option as ranges, including the cost of handling failed or repeat claims.
3. Add equity and access measures: the gap in processing time between groups, success at first attempt, and use by route.
4. Weigh options against the three parts of the strategic triangle, and consider reputational and political effects.
5. Pilot and publish results, with named measures and decision points.

## Decision

**Not yet decided.** This is a starting point. A team using it should replace this section with its own decision.

Suggested way to reach a decision:

* Confirm who decides, and what the departmental board needs to see.
* Agree criteria, including equity measures, and weights first.
* Gather the evidence above and record what was found.
* Share for comment publicly where appropriate, then decide and update the Status.

## Implications

If the team decides on a digital-first service with assisted routes, it would need to:

* Publish the measures and report on them regularly, including gaps between groups.
* Fund and train staff for assisted routes before launch.
* Co-design the assisted service with claimants who wait longest, and publish what was learned.
* Treat any proposal to close an office as a new decision based on the first year's data.
