# Phase in lean manufacturing

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2025-11-24
* Decision-maker(s): Plant Director
* Owner: Head of Operational Excellence

## Context

Our plant makes custom industrial enclosures on four production lines. Lead times average 31 days against a customer expectation of 20, work-in-progress stock fills much of the floor, and surveys of operators suggest large amounts of waiting, rework, and movement of materials. Leaders want to adopt lean manufacturing, which focuses on removing waste and delivering what the customer values, but a plant-wide change at once would disrupt orders we must deliver this year.

## Options considered

* **Change all four lines at once.** The fastest route to a consistent plant, but it risks the delivery of current orders and gives the operators no time to learn and adjust.
* **Phase in line by line, beginning with a pilot line.** Start with one line, map its value stream, reduce waste, and use the results to choose practices for the rest. Disruption is contained and operators see real results before their own line changes, but full adoption takes about 18 months and the lines work in different ways for a time.
* **Introduce lean tools plant-wide without redesigning the lines.** For example, 5S (sort, set in order, shine, standardize, sustain) everywhere. Visible and cheap, but it improves the appearance of the floor without addressing flow, so lead times would change little.

## Decision

We will phase in lean manufacturing line by line, beginning with line 2, which has the most repeat work. We will map the value stream, set a lead-time target for the line, and give the operators a role in choosing the improvements. We will start the next line only after the pilot line has held its lead time for a full quarter.

## Consequences

* The Head of Operational Excellence will lead the line 2 pilot and publish the value stream map and the results.
* Operators on the pilot line will be trained and will have time each week to propose and test improvements; we will fund that time.
* We will measure lead time, work-in-progress, rework, and on-time delivery on each line.
* During the rollout, some lines will work in the old way, and planning will need to take account of the differences.
* The pilot line's target is a lead time of 20 days; if we do not reach it we will find out why before extending to the next line.
