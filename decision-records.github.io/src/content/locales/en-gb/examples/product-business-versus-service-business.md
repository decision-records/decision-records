# Choose between a product business and a service business

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-07-08
* Decision-maker(s): CEO and board
* Owner: Chief Strategy Officer

## Context

We make most of our revenue (68%) from custom implementation projects for mid-sized customers, built on a software platform we own. Projects are profitable but unpredictable, and every one needs senior engineers, so growth is limited by hiring. Our platform is increasingly used without our services by partners, and investors are asking whether we are a product company or a services company. The answer changes what we hire, how we price, how we measure success, and what we say to customers.

## Options considered

* **Become a product business.** Sell the platform as a subscription with standard onboarding, and refer custom work to partners. Offers recurring revenue and growth that does not depend on headcount, but revenue would fall for roughly two years while we shift, and our best customers value the custom work.
* **Stay a service business.** Keep selling implementation projects and treat the platform as a tool for delivering them. Keeps the revenue and customer relationships we have, but leaves us exposed to hiring limits and to partners who deliver projects on our platform for less.
* **Run both, with separate economics.** Keep services as a distinct line with its own targets while the product line has its own roadmap, pricing, and measures. Keeps today's revenue while building a product business, but splits attention and risks leaving both lines under-invested.

## Decision

We will become a product business over three years. We will move to subscription pricing with standard onboarding, shift the customer-facing implementation work to a partner network in stages, and keep a small services team for strategic customers. We will review the plan annually against subscription revenue, gross margin, and customer retention.

## Consequences

* The Chief Strategy Officer will publish the three-year plan, including the order in which customer segments move to subscription pricing.
* We expect total revenue to dip in the first two years; the board has agreed to this and to the investment needed to cover it.
* Roles will change as implementation work moves to partners; a separate decision will record how we support the people affected.
* We will measure ourselves differently: annual recurring revenue, gross margin, and net revenue retention replace project utilisation as the headline measures.
* We give up control over some customers' implementation experience, in exchange for recurring revenue and growth that does not depend on hiring.
