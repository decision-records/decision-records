# Choose between a product business and a service business

* Status: request for comments
* Date: 2026-07-08
* Decision-maker(s): CEO and board (to be confirmed)
* Owner: Chief Strategy Officer

Summary: Work in progress. This is a starting point for a team that is deciding whether the company should become mainly a product business, stay mainly a services business, or run both. It shows how a team can frame the question, debate options, and gather information. It records no decision.

## Issue

A company earns most of its revenue from custom implementation projects built on a software platform it owns. Projects are profitable but unpredictable, each needs senior engineers, and growth is limited by hiring. Partners increasingly use the platform without the company's services, and investors ask whether it is a product company or a services company. The answer changes whom to hire, how to price, how to measure success, and what to tell customers.

The questions to answer:

* Where does the company actually create and capture value today, by customer and by offer?
* What do customers value in the custom work, and what would they accept instead?
* What would each model do to revenue, margin, growth, and risk over time, including during transition?
* Can partners deliver what customers need?

## Assumptions

Each assumption is something to test.

* **Customers would accept a more standard offer.** Test: interview a sample of customers across segments; separate those who need custom work from those who do not.
* **Partners can deliver to a standard customers accept.** Test: pilot with a few partners and measure outcomes.
* **Subscription revenue can replace project revenue.** Test: model scenarios using actual customer data, including how long the transition would take.
* **Investors and the board will accept a dip in revenue during transition.** Test: ask them directly, and show the scenarios.

## Constraints

* Strategic customers value the custom work and must not be abandoned.
* Revenue may dip during any transition.
* People in implementation roles would be affected; a separate decision is needed on how to support them.
* Unknown, to be confirmed: contract terms, partner capacity, and platform readiness for standard onboarding.

## Options considered

For each option, estimate value, costs, timeline, and capability needs, and note what evidence would change the team's mind.

* **Become a product business.** Sell the platform by subscription with standard onboarding, and refer custom work to partners.
  * Value: recurring revenue; growth less tied to hiring.
  * Cost drivers: product investment; partner programme; transition support; revenue dip.
  * Timeline shape: a multi-year transition in stages by customer segment.
  * Capabilities needed: product management; pricing; partner management; customer success.
* **Stay a service business.** Keep selling implementation projects, and treat the platform as a delivery tool.
  * Value: keeps current revenue and relationships.
  * Risks: growth stays limited by hiring; partners may deliver on the platform at lower cost.
* **Run both with separate economics.** Services and product are distinct lines with their own targets, roadmaps, and measures.
  * Value: keeps current revenue while building product revenue.
  * Risks: splits attention; both lines may be under-invested; internal conflict over priorities.
* **Productize selectively.** Package the most repeated custom work as standard offers, keeping custom work for large accounts.

## Argument

Points of debate the team should expect:

* **Identity.** What do we want to be known for, and what do our best people want to do?
* **Transition risk.** How long and deep is the revenue dip, and can we fund it?
* **Customer relationships.** Moving implementation to partners may weaken the direct relationship.
* **Measures.** Product businesses are judged on recurring revenue, retention, and margin; services businesses on utilisation and margin per project. Which will we hold ourselves to?

How the team could evaluate costs and trade-offs:

1. Segment current revenue and margin by customer and offer.
2. Build scenarios (best, likely, worst) for each model over a multi-year horizon, with transition costs, using ranges and stating assumptions.
3. Compare options on revenue quality, margin, growth potential, risk, capability needed, and effect on people and customers.
4. Identify early signals that would show whether the assumptions hold, such as partner delivery results and customer response to standard offers.
5. Run a pre-mortem and agree review points tied to named measures.

## Decision

**Not yet decided.** This is a starting point. A team using it should replace this section with its own decision.

Suggested way to reach a decision:

* Confirm who decides, and what the board needs.
* Agree criteria and weights first.
* Gather the evidence above and record what was found.
* Share for comment, then decide and update the Status.

## Implications

If the team decides to become a product business, it would need to:

* Publish a multi-year plan, including the order in which customer segments move.
* Explain the expected revenue dip, and secure agreement to it.
* Plan support for people whose roles change.
* Change headline measures to recurring revenue, margin, and retention.
* Review annually against those measures.
