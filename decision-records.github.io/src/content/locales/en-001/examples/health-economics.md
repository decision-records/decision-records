# Weigh health economics in a treatment funding decision

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-05-12
* Decision-maker(s): Regional commissioning board
* Owner: Director of Strategic Commissioning

## Context

A new medicine for a chronic condition affecting about 6,000 people in our region has been proposed for funding. Trial evidence shows a modest improvement in quality of life and a reduction in hospital admissions, at a price of 9,500 per patient per year. Our budget is fixed, so funding it means funding something else less. A decision based only on the price or only on the clinical benefit would ignore that trade-off. We need to weigh the cost-effectiveness, the effect on the budget, and equity.

## Options considered

* **Fund for all eligible patients.** Gives every eligible patient access and is simple to run, but the budget impact (about 57 million a year at full uptake) would require cuts elsewhere, and the cost per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) gained is above our usual threshold.
* **Do not fund.** No budget impact and no displacement of other services, but patients lose a treatment with a demonstrated benefit and the decision may increase inequality for those without other options.
* **Fund for a defined group with a managed-access agreement.** Fund for the patients who benefit most (those with the highest admission rates), with the supplier lowering the price, collecting outcome data, and reviewing in three years. Brings the cost per QALY nearer to the threshold and limits the budget impact, but it excludes some patients and depends on the supplier agreeing and on data we can actually collect.

## Decision

We will fund the medicine for the defined group under a managed-access agreement with a lower price, outcome data collection, and a review in three years. We make this decision on three considerations together: the incremental cost per QALY gained for the group, the budget impact over five years, and the effect on health inequalities.

## Consequences

* The Director of Strategic Commissioning will negotiate the agreement; if the supplier does not agree to the price and data terms, we will bring the decision back to the board.
* The budget impact is estimated at about 14 million a year at full uptake in the defined group; the board will name what is displaced, not leave it unstated.
* Patients outside the defined group will not be funded, and the eligibility criteria will be published with the reasons for them.
* We will measure admissions, quality of life, and uptake by deprivation group, and review the equity effect at the three-year review.
* We accept the risk that the group-level evidence is less certain than the overall trial evidence, and we will say so in the published rationale.
