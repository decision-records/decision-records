# Phase in Six Sigma

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-01-19
* Decision-maker(s): Chief Operating Officer
* Owner: Head of Quality

## Context

Our claims-processing operation handles 1.2 million cases a year, and about 7% of them need rework because of errors in data entry and routing. Rework costs an estimated 3.1 million a year and is a leading cause of customer complaints. Leaders want to use Six Sigma, the data-driven method for reducing variation and defects, to bring the rework rate down. Training everyone at once would take staff off the line, and our data is not yet good enough to measure defects consistently in every process.

## Options considered

* **Company-wide rollout with belt training for all managers.** Builds a shared method quickly, but removes many managers from operations for weeks and starts projects before we can measure them.
* **Phase in with a small group of trained practitioners and one process first.** Train a small group of Black Belts and Green Belts, choose one high-volume process (data entry), measure the defect rate, run a project, and use the result to choose the next. Slower, but it proves value with data and builds capability where it is needed.
* **Use outside consultants to run projects.** Fast, and experienced, but expensive and unlikely to leave internal capability behind.

## Decision

We will phase in Six Sigma starting with one process: data entry for new claims. A small group of internal staff will be trained as Green Belts and Black Belts and will run a DMAIC (define, measure, analyse, improve, control) project on that process. We will extend to routing and then to other processes only after the data entry project has met its target for three consecutive months.

## Consequences

* The Head of Quality will sponsor the training, select the belts, and report results to the COO.
* The first project's target is to cut data-entry rework from 7% to below 3.5%, measured in the same way before and after.
* We will first invest in consistent measurement, because without reliable defect data the method cannot work.
* Managers outside the pilot will not be asked to run projects until their process is selected; this limits the disruption to operations.
* We accept a slower start than a company-wide rollout, in exchange for evidence and capability that stay in the company.
