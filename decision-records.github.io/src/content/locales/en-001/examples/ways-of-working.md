# Adopt written ways of working

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-04-27
* Decision-maker(s): Head of Engineering
* Owner: Engineering Manager

## Context

Our product and engineering group has grown from 12 to 45 people in a year, and expectations that used to be understood are now different between teams: how quickly to answer a message, when a decision needs a meeting, how to disagree, and what counts as done. New joiners report that they learn these by making mistakes. We need ways of working that are explicit, understood, open to debate, and quick to flag when someone breaks them.

## Options considered

* **Rely on culture and onboarding conversations.** No effort, but it is the current approach, and it is already failing for people who join after the first cohort.
* **Adopt one company-wide set of ways of working.** One consistent set that everyone knows, but written by a few people and likely to feel imposed, and it would not suit the very different needs of design, engineering, and support.
* **Write a short group-wide set and let each team add its own working agreement.** A set of about ten shared principles and ground rules for the whole group, plus a team agreement that each team writes and reviews itself. Gives both consistency and ownership, but it takes team time and needs a place where agreements are kept and found.

## Decision

We will write a short set of group-wide ways of working (about ten principles and ground rules) and require each team to keep its own written working agreement alongside them. All of them will be kept in the team documentation, reviewed every quarter, and open to change by anyone through a proposal that the team discusses.

## Consequences

* The Engineering Manager will draft the group-wide set within a month, circulate it for comment, and then publish it.
* Each team will write its agreement in its first two team meetings after publication and keep it where new joiners will find it on their first day.
* Every agreement names who handles a breakage, so people know what to do when a rule is not followed.
* We will check twice a year that people know the agreements and can say how to change them.
* Teams spend some time writing and reviewing agreements, which we accept in exchange for fewer misunderstandings.
