# Phase in the Agile Manifesto

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-02-16
* Decision-maker(s): Chief Technology Officer
* Owner: Head of Delivery

## Context

Our delivery teams work in long, planned projects with fixed scope and hand-offs between analysis, build, and test. Releases happen about twice a year, and customers often receive something that no longer matches what they need. Leaders want to adopt the values and principles of the Agile Manifesto: individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, and responding to change. We have seen "agile transformations" fail when a ceremony is imposed without the underlying change, so we need a deliberate way to introduce it.

## Options considered

* **Mandate a single agile method for all teams from next quarter.** Quickest and most consistent, but it focuses on ceremonies, and teams that are not ready would perform them without changing how they work.
* **Phase in by team, starting with volunteers.** Start with two teams that want to try it, release in short cycles, and let them show the results; the manifesto's values are the guide and the specific practices are chosen by each team. Slower and uneven across the company, but it builds real examples and keeps the practices tied to the values.
* **Hire an outside coaching firm to transform all teams.** Brings experience and speed, but it is expensive and tends to leave when the engagement ends, with little capability inside the company.

## Decision

We will phase in the Agile Manifesto by team: two volunteer teams in the first quarter, four more in the second, and the rest when the first six have run for two full quarters. Each team chooses its own practices, and we judge them against the manifesto's values and by delivery of working software to customers every two to four weeks.

## Consequences

* The Head of Delivery will support the volunteer teams, collect what they learn, and report to the CTO each quarter.
* Teams that have not yet moved will continue to work in the current way, so the organization will run two ways of working for about a year.
* Commitments to customers in fixed-scope contracts will not change automatically; we will handle each contract as it comes up for renewal.
* We will measure the time from an idea to working software in customers' hands, customer feedback, and team satisfaction, not the number of agile ceremonies.
* If the first teams do not show improvement after two quarters, we will stop and record what we learned before going further.
