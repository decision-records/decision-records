# Adopt project portfolio management

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-10-05
* Decision-maker(s): Executive team
* Owner: Head of the Project Management Office

## Context

We run about 90 active projects across six departments, and each department approves, staffs, and reports on its own. Leaders cannot see the total demand on shared people, such as our 14 data engineers, who are named on projects whose combined requests add up to more than twice their capacity. Projects stall waiting for the same few people, and low-value projects survive because no one compares them with the alternatives. We need a way to decide which projects to start, continue, or stop, based on strategy and capacity across the whole organization.

## Options considered

* **Keep departmental decisions.** No new process and full local autonomy, but it leaves the capacity conflicts and the lack of comparison unresolved.
* **Create a central project portfolio office that approves every project.** One view and consistent criteria, but every project would wait on one team, which becomes a bottleneck, and departments would feel the loss of control.
* **Adopt portfolio management with a light central process.** One shared list of projects with common scoring criteria (strategic fit, value, cost, risk, and the capacity needed), a quarterly portfolio review by the executive team to start, continue, or stop projects, and a rule that only projects above a set size need review. Gives one view and trade-offs made deliberately, without routing small work through a central team, but it needs reliable data from every department and a decision-making meeting that actually decides.

## Decision

We will adopt project portfolio management with a light central process: one shared list of all projects above a set size (more than 200 person-days or 100,000 in cost), common scoring criteria, and a quarterly executive portfolio review that decides which projects to start, continue, or stop. Smaller projects stay under departmental control.

## Consequences

* The Head of the Project Management Office will keep the portfolio list, publish the scoring criteria within six weeks, and prepare each quarterly review.
* Each department will register its projects and its capacity by role, so the review sees demand against capacity and not demand alone.
* The executive team commits to deciding at the review, including stopping projects; stopped projects will be recorded with the reason.
* Departments give up some independence over larger projects in exchange for shared capacity being used on the highest-value work.
* We will review the process after two cycles, and measure the share of projects delivered on time, the number stopped early, and the use of shared teams.
