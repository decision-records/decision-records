# Usage-based pricing

* Status: request for comments
* Date: 2026-04-05
* Decision-maker(s): VP Product, with CFO input (to be confirmed)
* Owner: Head of Pricing & Packaging

Summary: Work in progress. This is a starting point for a team that is considering whether to change from flat per-seat pricing to a model that charges for usage, or a mix of the two. It shows how a team can frame the question, debate the options, and gather information. It records no decision.

## Issue

A software product is priced per seat. The team suspects that this charges light users more than the value they receive, and so contributes to their leaving, while under-charging customers who use the product heavily. Competitors have been moving to usage-based or hybrid pricing, and prospects are asking why the company has not.

The questions to answer:

* What is the product's value metric: what unit of usage tracks the value a customer gets?
* Do light and heavy customers really behave and feel the way we think?
* Which pricing model best matches value while keeping revenue predictable enough to plan?
* How would existing customers move to a new model fairly?

## Assumptions

Each assumption is something to test.

* **Usage is a good proxy for value.** Test: compare usage with customer outcomes and satisfaction across a range of accounts.
* **Light users leave because price feels too high.** Test: review cancellation reasons and talk to departed light users.
* **Heavy users will pay more for more value.** Test: ask a sample about willingness to pay, and check how sensitive they are to price changes.
* **We can meter and bill usage accurately and transparently.** Test: check data quality, and ask customers whether they can predict their bill.

## Constraints

* No existing customer should face a net price increase without advance notice.
* Sales compensation plans must reflect the new structure before it is used for new deals.
* Finance needs forecasting models that can cope with revenue that varies with usage.
* Unknown, to be confirmed: contract terms that fix prices, what the billing system can do, and competitor pricing details.

## Options considered

For each option, estimate value, costs, timeline, and capability needs, and note what evidence would change the team's mind.

* **Keep flat per-seat pricing.**
  * Value: simple; no migration.
  * Risks: leaves the churn and under-charging issues; competitors differ.
* **Move fully to usage-based pricing.**
  * Value: price closely follows value; low barrier to start.
  * Cost drivers: metering, billing and reporting systems; support for bill questions; sales changes.
  * Risks: revenue volatility; customers who dislike unpredictable bills.
* **Hybrid: a base fee plus usage-based charges.**
  * Value: more predictable revenue than pure usage pricing, while still addressing light and heavy users.
  * Cost drivers: as above, plus design complexity.
  * Timeline shape: new customers first; existing customers moved after a protected period.
* **Tiered plans with usage limits.** A simpler middle path that may meet most of the need.
* **Experiment first**, offering the new model to a subset of new customers or a segment, and compare.

## Argument

Points of debate the team should expect:

* **Predictability versus fairness.** Customers and finance value predictable bills; usage pricing feels fairer but varies.
* **Which metric?** Seats, calls, records, outcomes? A poor metric creates the wrong behaviour.
* **Migration.** Grandfathering protects customers but delays benefits and complicates operations.
* **Competition.** Following competitors is not a reason in itself. What does our evidence say?

How the team could evaluate costs and trade-offs:

1. Model revenue under each option using existing customer data: for each account, what would it pay under the new model? Show the winners and losers, and the spread, as ranges.
2. Estimate price elasticity qualitatively and through tests, and consider customer reaction to bill changes.
3. Cost the change: metering, billing, support, sales training, legal and contract changes, forecasting tools.
4. Compare the options for revenue level, revenue stability, customer fairness, growth effects, and complexity; test sensitivity to assumptions.
5. Plan an experiment and define in advance what results would lead to scaling it, adjusting it, or stopping.

## Decision

**Not yet decided.** This is a starting point. A team using it should replace this section with its own decision.

Suggested way to reach a decision:

* Confirm who decides and who must sign off (for example Finance).
* Agree criteria and weights first.
* Gather the evidence above and record what was found.
* Share for comment, then decide and update the Status.

## Implications

If the team decides to move to a hybrid model, it would need to:

* Model every existing account to confirm no one faces a surprise increase.
* Update sales compensation and train sales and support.
* Revise revenue forecasting to handle variation.
* Contact at-risk light-usage customers early, since they should see their bill fall.
* Decide separately the exact overage tiers and thresholds.
